   1. Pragmatics studies the meaning that linguistic expressions receive in use.
   2. Pragmatics studies only dictionary meanings and ignores context.

   4. The same sentence can have different communicative functions in different situations.
   5. Pragmatics can include the idea that utterances perform actions.

   1. Sharon's answer "Ministry" is not informative enough for Kiki at first.

   3. Kiki can identify the referent after Sharon gives further clarification.
   4. This example is related to assigning reference.
   5. There is a gap between the dictionary meaning of "Ministry" and the intended referent in context.

3. Speech acts에 대한 설명으로 옳은 것을 모두 고르시오.
   1. A locutionary act concerns what was literally said.
   2. An illocutionary act concerns what the speaker meant to do with the utterance.
   3. A perlocutionary act concerns what actually happened as a result of the utterance.
   4. Austin and Searle are associated with the view that language is a form of action.
   5. Speech act theory explains every utterance only by judging whether it is true or false.

4. "여기 좀 춥지 않아?"라는 발화에 대한 분석으로 옳은 것을 모두 고르시오.

   2. In context, the utterance can function as a request to turn up the heat.
   3. If the listener turns on the stove, that result can be analyzed as a perlocutionary act.
   4. If an utterance has an interrogative form, its function is always a request for information.
   5. The speaker's intention can be inferred from context.

5. High-context culture의 특징으로 옳은 것을 모두 고르시오.
   1. Much information is not expressed directly in words but is carried by context.
   2. Long-term relationships and loyalty tend to be important.

   4. Shared background knowledge and relationships play an important role in understanding.

   3. High-context culture can be associated with understanding without saying everything directly.
   4. Low-context culture can emphasize that if one is thinking clearly, one should be able to say it.
   5. High-context culture always makes precise feedback and orderly explanation easier.
